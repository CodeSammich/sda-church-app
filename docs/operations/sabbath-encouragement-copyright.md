# Sabbath Encouragement: attribution and copyright review

## Attribution and permitted ministry use

The Sabbath Encouragement pages quote **Ellen G. White**, but they are not exclusively
Ellen White material. They may also contain Bible quotations, editorial headings, devotional
material, and other attributed or unattributed source text. Preserve the original work title,
volume or edition, and page citation wherever an Ellen White quotation is printed, and do not
attribute the entire page to her. The Chinese source snapshot is `sabbath_encouragement.pdf`,
stored in the church's Google Drive and mapped in `SabbathEncouragement.gs` so that page 20
corresponds to the Sabbath of 2026-08-22. A backup copy is kept in
[`public/library/`](../../public/library/sabbath_encouragement.pdf), where the app's library also
offers it to readers. Its author metadata was removed before it was added to this public
repository.

**Provenance.** The Chinese compilation was edited by churches in China and has been passed
freely from church to church there. The pastor reports that those churches checked it for copyright
and treat it as free to share
([#248](https://github.com/New-York-Chinese-Seventh-day-Adventist/sda-church-app/issues/248#issuecomment-5852167793)). This is secondhand information: it comes through
the pastor, not from the compilers directly.

That covers the compilation itself: its selection, arrangement, and headings. The Ellen G. White
quotations in it are Chinese translations of her books, and the rights to those translations may
belong to their publishers rather than to the compilers. The church's use of those excerpts is
non-commercial ministry use, weighed under the fair use factors below. If a rights holder objects,
remove `public/library/sabbath_encouragement.pdf` and its library entry.

**What this means for an English translation.** A direct English translation would add only the
translation itself. Its quotations would come from Ellen G. White's original English and from the
Bible, and the compilation it follows is shared without restriction by the churches that made it.
So an English translation made by this church should carry no outside rights. This is one more data
point for the English translation tracked in #248, not a legal opinion. The cautions below about
posthumous Ellen White compilations still apply to any quotation taken from one. That translation
hasn't been made yet (#248 is open); until it is, the printed bulletin's English column is a labeled
machine translation from the Chinese. See [What the printed bulletin does today](#what-the-printed-bulletin-does-today).

This material is included solely for the **non-commercial ministry use of New York Chinese
Seventh-day Adventist Church** in its printed bulletins and companion mobile app. It is not
being sold, licensed, or offered as a substitute for the cited books. The church should not
remove attribution, imply endorsement by the Ellen G. White Estate, or reuse a modern
translation, website transcription, typography, or compilation outside the church's ministry
workflow without confirming permission.

This notice records a practical source review; it is not legal advice and is not a copyright
clearance or permission from the Ellen G. White Estate, a publisher, or a periodical owner.

## What can and cannot be assumed

The U.S. Copyright Office explains that works published in the United States before January 1,
1931 are in the public domain. Works first published from 1923 through 1963 require a
publication-date and renewal investigation; age alone is not enough for every edition.

Translations, later editions, editorial selections, annotations, page design, and website
transcriptions can have separate copyrights even when the underlying historical text is old.

The independent Ellen White Research corpus states that its underlying published texts are
public domain, but it also says that its software, design, and selection/arrangement are not
being released as public-domain material. This repository uses that kind of corpus only as a
research aid for identifying original wording. It must not copy its site layout or database
presentation.

The White Estate's own legal notice reserves rights in the content of its websites and apps
and allows only fair use of excerpts. Its estate information also says that copyright in
unpublished manuscripts resides with the trustees. Accordingly, a citation to an old date or
to a manuscript is not, by itself, permission to reproduce the material.

## What the printed bulletin does today

The Brooklyn printed bulletin prints one page of the compilation each week, side by side in
Chinese and English (`google-apps-script/SabbathEncouragement.gs`). The script holds a text
snapshot of all 52 pages. The Chinese column is the original. The compilation has no
original English edition, so the English column is a machine translation from the Chinese,
labeled as one. The table above records how each source should be treated; the script
treats every source the same way:

- **Chinese column:** the page's text from the snapshot, unchanged apart from line breaks,
  including its Chinese Bible quotations and citations.
- **English column:** each paragraph is machine translated from Chinese by Google's
  `LanguageApp`. That includes the Ellen White quotations, their citations, and the headings, so
  her words appear as a translation from the Chinese rather than in her original English
  wording, which the label says. This applies to every source in the snapshot,
  including the passages it cites to 怀氏文稿 (manuscripts), 儿童指导, and 证言精选.
- **Bible quotations in the English column:** a quotation in 「」 or “” followed by a Chinese
  reference the script recognizes, such as (创 2:1-3), is set aside before translation. It is
  replaced by the BSB text of that reference, fetched from the HelloAO Bible API and printed as
  “…” (Genesis 2:1-3, BSB). If the BSB text can't be fetched, the column prints
  `[BSB text unavailable: …]` in its place. A quotation not in that form is machine translated
  with the rest of the paragraph; in the current snapshot, page 2's Hebrews 4:4, whose quotation
  marks don't match, is one.
- **Label:** under the English heading, the column prints "Machine-translated from Chinese; may
  not be fully accurate. Bible quotations use the Berean Standard Bible (BSB)."

## Fair-use analysis for this project

US fair use is a case-specific analysis under 17 U.S.C. §107. The four factors are:

1. purpose and character of the use;
2. nature of the copyrighted work;
3. amount and substantiality used; and
4. effect on the potential market.

The church's non-commercial ministry purpose supports the first factor. It does not create an
automatic ministry or religious exemption. Reproducing the entirety of a 52-page compilation,
especially posthumous selections or unpublished material, can weigh against fair use under the
amount and substantiality factors and may affect the market for authorized books, translations,
or compilations. A short quotation with attribution is a different analysis from embedding the
whole source in a public app and printing it every week.

For that reason, the repository should follow this order:

1. Use a verified pre-1931 English edition when the underlying work and exact edition are clear.
2. For a posthumous compilation, replace the quotation with its original cited source when that
   source can be identified and verified.
3. Exclude manuscript-derived passages unless the church obtains written permission or a
   licensed published version.
4. If the church wants the complete verbatim 52-page English corpus regardless of those limits,
   obtain a written license or legal review before deploying it in the public mobile app.
